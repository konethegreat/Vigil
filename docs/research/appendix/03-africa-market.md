*For the Vigil founding team (MB Tech). Research cut-off: 25 Sep 2026. US$ figures use ~R16.2/US$, the rate implied by World Cargo News' Sep-2026 conversions ([WCN](https://www.worldcargonews.com/news/2026/09/transnet-returns-to-profit-as-revenue-rises-7-1/)). ## Executive summary

- **The pain has moved from power generation to the edge of the grid.** There has been no load-shedding for 490 days (to 18 Sep 2026). But municipalities owed Eskom **R120.26bn (US$7.4bn)** on 31 Jul 2026, projected to reach **R358bn by FY2031** ([TimesLive](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-09-23-municipalities-owe-eskom-r120bn-as-debt-threatens-viability/); [IOL](https://iol.co.za/business/2026-09-23-municipal-debt-to-eskom-could-explode-to-r358-billion-by-2031-ramokgopa-warns/)).
- **Logistics is the biggest drag, and it is in a reform window.** Logistics failures cost about **R353bn in 2023** ([Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/business/transnet-costing-south-africa-r1-billion-day/)). Now 12 private train operators have access agreements, ICTSI runs part of Durban's port, the Border Management Authority (BMA) is rebuilding six border posts for R12.5bn, and a Transport Economic Regulator starts in April 2027. Each reform creates new buyers with new data needs.
- **Infrastructure crime taxes every sector.** Transnet spends **about R4bn a year on security** and still loses **4.5 million tonnes of freight a year to cable theft** ([Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-09-07-transnet-turnaround-losses-narrow-but-we-have-a-long-way-to-go-says-ceo/)). Copper theft costs the economy **more than R45bn a year** ([GCIS](https://www.gcis.gov.za/content/newsroom/blogs/2025-12/metals-theft)).
- **Procurement integrity is the governance failure that runs through it all.** Municipalities have run up **R145.21bn** of irregular expenditure since 2021-22, 87% of it from breaking procurement rules ([FWdK](https://fwdeklerk.org/auditor-general-tsakani-maluleke-tabled-the-consolidated-report-on-local-government-audit-outcomes-2024-25-on-24-june-2026/)).
- **Recommended first wedge: "Corridor Integrity" for Transnet and TRIM**, its new rail infrastructure manager. It is one data model joining rail assets, incidents, security contractors, procurement and court cases on two or three corridors, with results written back into maintenance, dispatch and prosecution workflows. Build the procurement and counterparty graph first as the shared foundation, and sell it on its own to private buyers for early revenue.

## 1. Energy and water (South Africa)

### Facts
**Load-shedding history and status.**
- **2023 was the worst year:** about 7,000 hours and 16,000 GWh shed, against about 4,000 hours and 8,000 GWh in 2022 ([EN](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/graphics-confirm-2023-as-loadsheddings-annus-horribilis-2023-11-27)). The Reserve Bank put Stage 6 at about R899m of lost output a day ([Moneyweb](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/south-africa/load-shedding-may-cost-sa-r899m-a-day-sarb-says/)).
- **Status on 18 Sep 2026:** 490 days without load-shedding (since 16 May 2025). The fleet's availability factor is 67.96% year to date, a six-year high. The diesel turbines' load factor fell to 1.06% from 7.25% and diesel spending fell by R4.8bn ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/eaf-reaches-67-96-diesel-expenditure-falls-by-r4-8-billion-emissions-outperform-target-and-load-reduction-declines-to-just-3-4-of-customers/)).
- **Unplanned outages fell to 19.47% from 34.48%** ([Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/eskom-powers-through-winter-476-days-without-load-shedding/)).
- **Targeted cuts continue.** "Load reduction" still hits 247,617 customers (3.4%) in areas with high losses ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/eaf-reaches-67-96-diesel-expenditure-falls-by-r4-8-billion-emissions-outperform-target-and-load-reduction-declines-to-just-3-4-of-customers/)).

**Unbundling and the new market.**
- **The Electricity Regulation Amendment Act has been in force since 1 Jan 2025** ([Polity](https://www.polity.org.za/article/south-africas-energy-outlook-key-developments-from-2025-and-what-to-expect-in-2026-2026-01-29-1)).
- **The transmission company NTCSA has traded since 1 Jul 2024.** It holds 33,199 km of lines ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transmission_Company_of_South_Africa)).
- **The wholesale market (SAWEM) has slipped.** The market-operator licence came in Dec 2025 and the Market Code was filed on 25 Feb 2026, but SAWEM missed its 1 Apr 2026 launch ([EN](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/launch-of-sawem-delayed-but-ntcsa-says-preparations-are-well-advanced-2026-04-10)).
- **Only part of the market is live.** Internal trading began in April 2026 with 32 participants; external trading is targeted for "early 2027". Government backs a fully independent, state-owned transmission system operator, with the Phase 2 plan due Oct 2026 ([Operation Vulindlela Q1 2026/27](https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2026/Operation%20Vulindlela%20Progress%20Report%20Q1%20-%202026.pdf)).

**Transmission build-out.**
- **The plan needs 14,500 km of new lines in a decade,** against 33,000 km built in a century. The transmission plan is worth R440bn (US$27bn), with a R134bn five-year funding gap; about 30% is meant to be privately built ([Business Day](https://www.businessday.co.za/companies/2026-08-05-ntcsa-seeks-r134bn-to-fast-track-transmission-grid-expansion/)).
- **The first private round is 1,164 km** of 400 kV line, backed by a credit-guarantee vehicle built with the World Bank ([Treasury](https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2025/2025060601%20Media%20Statement%20-%20Independent%20Transmission%20Programme.pdf)).
- **Eskom plans R320bn of capital spending over five years** ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/continued-execution-of-turnaround-plan-drives-eskom-groups-profitability-and-sustainability-profit-after-tax-increases-to-r24-3-billion-unaudited-in-first-six-months-of-fy2026-to-be-reinv/)).

**Municipal arrear debt to Eskom.**
- **R120.26bn was overdue on 31 Jul 2026,** up from R111.6bn in March ([TimesLive](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-09-23-municipalities-owe-eskom-r120bn-as-debt-threatens-viability/)).
- **Few municipalities keep to relief terms.** Eighty-three are in formal debt recovery; 71 are in Treasury's relief programme, and only 10 of those comply.
- **Eskom is writing off revenue.** It left R15.8bn (4.5% of revenue) unrecognised in FY2026 because it doesn't expect to collect it (same source).

**Losses to theft ("non-technical" losses).**
- **About 7.9 TWh, roughly 8% of sales, is stolen:** about **R17.5bn (US$1.1bn)** a year ([Eskom, Nov 2025](https://www.eskom.co.za/continued-execution-of-turnaround-plan-drives-eskom-groups-profitability-and-sustainability-profit-after-tax-increases-to-r24-3-billion-unaudited-in-first-six-months-of-fy2026-to-be-reinv/)).
- **Measured losses were R11.17bn from Apr 2025 to Feb 2026:** R8.15bn from theft and R3.02bn technical, with R500m recovered ([DFA](https://dfa.co.za/diamond-fields-advertiser/south-african/2026-05-18-eskom-battles-r11bn-in-electricity-losses-as-theft-crackdown-recovers-r500m/)).
- **Gauteng loses about R7bn a year and Soweto about R3m a day** ([EWN, Jun 2025](https://www.ewn.co.za/2025/06/07/eskom-says-r7bn-lost-annually-due-to-illegal-connections-non-technical-issues-in-gauteng)).

**Smart meters.**
- **513,648 are installed**, 89% of a 577,347 priority target. The target is 1.69m customers by Mar 2027 ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/eaf-reaches-67-96-diesel-expenditure-falls-by-r4-8-billion-emissions-outperform-target-and-load-reduction-declines-to-just-3-4-of-customers/)).
- **The rollout was far behind in Jan 2026.** Only 73,523 were in, and Eskom could not cost the full 6.2m-meter rollout ([ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/eskom-87-short-of-2027-smart-meter-target/rW1xLv5n89a7Rk6m)).

**Cable theft and vandalism.**
- **Eskom's booked losses fell to R221m** (Apr 2024–Feb 2025) from R271m ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/theft-and-vandalism-of-eskoms-infrastructure-remain-a-major-threat-to-continuous-electricity-supply-and-public-safety/)).
- **The wider impact is far larger.** Copper theft costs Eskom R5–7bn a year plus about R2bn in replacements, and the economy more than R45bn (Genesis Analytics) ([GCIS](https://www.gcis.gov.za/content/newsroom/blogs/2025-12/metals-theft)).

**Water.**
- **The 2025 "Drop" audits (released Mar 2026):**
  - **47.3% of water is lost or unbilled**, against a ~30% benchmark.
  - **47% of 848 wastewater systems are critical**, up from 39% in 2021/22, and only 14 are certified.
  - **7.9% of drinking-water systems are critical-risk** ([EN](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/new-drop-reports-shows-more-deterioration-of-south-africas-wastewater-systems-2026-04-01)).
- **The Auditor-General (AGSA) found R18.98bn lost in 2023/24.** Of that, R14.89bn came from leaks, illegal connections and billing failures, and R2.32bn went on tankers; Gauteng accounted for R6.9bn ([Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-04-27-water-crisis-close-to-r19bn-lost-that-could-have-brought-water-into-homes-/)).
- **Johannesburg is in acute difficulty:**
  - 49% of its water is lost or unbilled, and pipe bursts run at 4,108 a month (2,960 a year earlier).
  - The City took R3.4bn out of Johannesburg Water's account on 30 Jun 2026.
  - A Presidential Johannesburg Working Group exists, and a national Water Crisis Committee has been proposed ([DM](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-07-28-city-drains-r34bn-from-johannesburg-water-leaving-entity-stranded-and-projects-stalled/)).
- **Underinvestment is long-running.** Treasury cites more than R25bn of underinvestment in Johannesburg's system ([PMG, Nov 2025](https://pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/42068/)).
- **Rand Water's maintenance left some residents 12 days without water** (Jun 2026) ([DM](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-06-09-joburg-residents-go-12-days-without-water-after-maintenance-programme/)).

**Distressed municipalities.**
- **AGSA's 2024-25 local-government audit (24 Jun 2026):**
  - **Only 39 clean audits (15%)**, none in a metro ([SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/south-africa/ag-tables-local-government-outcome-reports-2024-2025)).
  - **R145.21bn of irregular expenditure** since 2021-22.
  - **R14.73bn of water losses and R21.63bn of electricity losses.**
  - **45% of municipalities adopted unfunded budgets** ([FWdK](https://fwdeklerk.org/auditor-general-tsakani-maluleke-tabled-the-consolidated-report-on-local-government-audit-outcomes-2024-25-on-24-june-2026/)).
- **The local-government ministry (CoGTA) rated 66 municipalities dysfunctional and 163 distressed out of 257** (2023) ([Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/politics/almost-90-of-municipalities-are-distressed-or-dysfunctional/)).

### Analysis
Generation is largely fixed; the grid edge is not. There is about R36bn (US$2.2bn) of reported municipal distribution losses plus R120bn of arrears.

At root this is a matching problem: linking each meter to a premises, account, token vendor, municipality and payment. But municipal payers are insolvent and political. The buyer with money is **Eskom Distribution**; Eskom made **R24.3bn profit in the first half of FY2026** ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/continued-execution-of-turnaround-plan-drives-eskom-groups-profitability-and-sustainability-profit-after-tax-increases-to-r24-3-billion-unaudited-in-first-six-months-of-fy2026-to-be-reinv/)). Water pain is extreme, but Johannesburg Water cannot even keep its own cash. Utilities are an expansion market, not the wedge.

## 2. Logistics and trade corridors

### Facts
**Rail.**
- **Freight rail volumes are recovering slowly:**
  - 149.5 million tonnes (mt) in 2022/23 ([EN](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/transnet-expects-to-report-rail-volumes-of-168-mt-in-202526-with-reforms-key-to-meeting-250-mt-goal-2026-03-24)).
  - 151.7 mt in 2023/24 and 160.1 mt in 2024/25 ([DM](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-09-07-transnet-turnaround-losses-narrow-but-we-have-a-long-way-to-go-says-ceo/)).
  - **167.9 mt in 2025/26 (+4.9%)**, still short of the 180 mt goal ([Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/new-version-of-rail-network-statement-officially-gazetted-2026-09-21)).
- **The target is 250 mt by 2030;** the peak was about 226 mt in 2017/18 **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **216 of 481 CRRC locomotives were idle** for lack of spares ([EN](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/transnet-expects-to-report-rail-volumes-of-168-mt-in-202526-with-reforms-key-to-meeting-250-mt-goal-2026-03-24)).

**Crime on the network.**
- **Security incidents are falling but still high:** 6,345 in 2024/25, down 23%.
- **Security spending isn't stopping the losses.** Transnet spends **"nearly R4bn" (about US$250m) a year on security yet loses 4.5 mt a year to cable theft**. The CEO says cutting that by a third would make freight profitable ([DM](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-09-07-transnet-turnaround-losses-narrow-but-we-have-a-long-way-to-go-says-ceo/)).
- **Incidents fell a further 7% in 2025/26** ([WCN](https://www.worldcargonews.com/news/2026/09/transnet-returns-to-profit-as-revenue-rises-7-1/)).
- **1,121 km of cable was stolen in FY2023** ([GCIS](https://www.gcis.gov.za/content/newsroom/blogs/2025-12/metals-theft)) **and 1,013 km in FY2024** ([TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/crime-problem-crippling-eskom-transnet/251040/)).
- **Fuel is stolen from the pipelines too:** 3m litres in 50 incidents in 2021, on a 3,114 km network carrying about 16bn litres a year ([Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/transnets-big-oil-heist-over-3-million-litres-of-fuel-stolen-this-year-alone/)). We found no totals for 2025–26 **[UNVERIFIED]**.

**Transnet finances (FY2025/26).**
- **The headline result:** profit of R4.6bn, revenue of R88.6bn and 4.58m containers (twenty-foot equivalents, TEU).
- **One-off gain:** the result includes a **R12.5bn profit on selling** a 49.999% Durban terminal stake to ICTSI for R10.5bn ([WCN](https://www.worldcargonews.com/news/2026/09/transnet-returns-to-profit-as-revenue-rises-7-1/)).
- *Analysis:* without the one-off, operations lost money, so any security spending must prove its return.

Cape Town's ship turnaround fell from 103 hours (2023/24) to 74 hours (2025/26) ([IOL](https://iol.co.za/capetimes/news/2026-06-19-cape-town-stays-bottom-of-global-port-rankings-despite-gains/)).

**What logistics failures cost.**
- **Stellenbosch University (Havenga) with the GAIN Group:** about **R411bn in 2022 and R353bn in 2023**, roughly R1bn a day. Coal lost R88bn of foreign earnings in 2022 ([Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/business/transnet-costing-south-africa-r1-billion-day/)).
- **The Minerals Council:** R150bn of mineral exports that didn't happen in 2022 ([SABC](https://www.sabcnews.com/sabcnews/transnets-rail-inefficiencies-cost-r150-billion-in-unrealised-mineral-exports/)).

**Reform and private participation.**
- **The Freight Logistics Roadmap (Dec 2023)** is steered by the National Logistics Crisis Committee (NLCC), a Presidency–business body ([Polity](https://www.polity.org.za/article/operation-vulindlela-opening-the-road-2026-09-04)). By April 2026, 60% of the freight reforms were late ([EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/04/23/operation-vulindlela-60-freight-logistics-sector-reforms-delayed)).
- **ICTSI took over Durban's Pier 2 container terminal** for 25 years from 1 Jan 2026, targeting 2.0m→2.8m TEU ([ICTSI](https://www.ictsi.com/press-releases/transnet-and-ictsi-seal-partnership-dct-pier-2)).
- **Eleven private operators have rail access agreements.** They are ARC SA, Barberry, Grindrod, Interlinks, IRACEMA, Menar, Minrail, Motheo, Sharp, The Railway Corporation and TLD Marine/MSC, for +24 mt. A leasing company (LeaseCo) will provide about 500 locomotives and 17,000 wagons ([EN](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/eleven-private-train-operators-gear-up-for-mainline-entry-after-concluding-access-agreements-2026-05-13)).
- **The fourth rail network statement (the track-access rules) was gazetted 21 Sep 2026.** It covers 12 operators, 15-year contracts, ad hoc slots and "improved visibility of available capacity". Pilots run before end-2026 and mainline service from 2027 ([Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/new-version-of-rail-network-statement-officially-gazetted-2026-09-21)).
- **The Transport Economic Regulator becomes fully operational in April 2027** ([OV](https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2026/Operation%20Vulindlela%20Progress%20Report%20Q1%20-%202026.pdf)).

**Border posts.**
- **Stellenbosch (2026) puts border delays at up to R16bn (US$1bn) a year:**
  - R1,090–1,258 per truck-hour.
  - About 68,000 truck-hours lost a week at four crossings.
  - The worst 5% of northbound Beitbridge crossings take 57 hours ([Business Report](https://businessreport.co.za/economy/2026-07-28-border-delays-threaten-sas-trade-ambitions-as-congestion-costs-economy-up-to-r16-billion-a-year/)).
- **Business Unity SA's freight snapshot (15 Feb 2026):**
  - Beitbridge (SA to Zimbabwe) queue 28.7 hours; Groblersbrug 12.5 hours; Lebombo 3.4 hours at about 1,300 trucks a day.
  - Beitbridge and Kasumbalesa about 24 hours, the worst in SADC.
  - Delays cost **about R392m (US$24.5m) that week** ([BUSA #269](https://southafrica.savinodelbene.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/BUSA-Cargo-Movement-Update-2026-02-15.pdf)).
- **Chirundu takes about 39 hours northbound.** A US$66.8m upgrade is due to close financing in Sep 2026 ([Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/analysis/chirundu-border-upgrade-bot-concession/)).
- **The BMA is rebuilding six posts for R12.5bn:** Beitbridge, Lebombo, Oshoek, Maseru Bridge, Kopfontein and Ficksburg, which carry more than 80% of flows. They become one-stop border posts with "real-time coordination between agencies", with construction from late 2026 ([Freight News](https://www.freightnews.co.za/article/south-african-land-border-posts-get-r125bn-overhaul)).
- **Process fixes work.** An Oshoek time study cut crossings from 9 hours to 20 minutes ([defenceWeb](https://defenceweb.co.za/border-security-2/bma-outlines-technological-revolution-at-border-management-conference-2025/)). A 2023 failure in SARS's customs system caused a queue of more than 1,100 trucks at Lebombo ([Freight News](https://www.freightnews.co.za/article/lebombo-ore-truck-queue-grows-more-1100-tippers)).

**Regional corridors and the African free-trade area (AfCFTA).**
- **Lobito corridor (Angola, DRC, Zambia):**
  - The rail concession went to Lobito Atlantic Railway (Mota-Engil, Trafigura, Vecturis) in 2022.
  - The first DRC copper reached the US in Aug 2024.
  - China signed a US$1.4bn deal to modernise the rival TAZARA line in Nov 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobito_Corridor)).
  - The new 830 km Zambia line has US$500m from the Africa Finance Corporation (AFC) and US$200m from the African Development Bank (AfDB), cutting transit from 16 days to 7 ([Further Africa](https://furtherafrica.com/2026/04/30/lobito-rail-corridor-gets-afc-afdb-backing/)).
- **Other African ports in the CPPI 2024:** Tanger Med 5, Dakar 108, Lagos 322, Maputo 347, Dar es Salaam 360, Mombasa 375, Walvis Bay 376 ([Ecofin](https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites/2309-131689-indice-cppi-2024-dakar-meilleur-port-a-conteneurs-d-afrique-subsaharienne)).
- **The AfCFTA has barely moved intra-African trade:**
  - It is about 16% of the continent's trade (US$210bn in 2025).
  - About 60% of trade costs come from problems behind the border.
  - Goods wait about 126 hours in customs on average ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/09/08/afcfta-at-five-the-3-4-trillion-market-africa-still-wont-trade-in/)).

**Cargo crime.**
- **9,068 truck hijackings from Jan 2021 to Dec 2025,** costing the economy R2–6bn a year. Security has risen from 0.3% to 2.3% of hauliers' operating costs ([Crown](https://www.crown.co.za/capital-equipment-news/transport-freight-and-logistics/36870-the-truck-hijacking-crisis-costs-the-economy-billions-a-year)).
- **Police (SAPS) figures for Jan–Mar 2026: 347 truck hijackings,** down 21.6%, 64% of them in Gauteng ([Digit FMS/SAPS](https://digitfms.co.za/news/saps-crime-statistics-fleet-security-q4-2026/)).

### Analysis
Logistics is the best-funded problem with the clearest return on investment. The 2026–27 reforms create **new, solvent, private buyers**: the private rail operators, ICTSI, LeaseCo, the border concessionaires and mining exporters. They all need a **neutral, auditable data layer that several parties can share**.

Open access turns capacity, availability, incident and penalty data into contract terms. TRIM becomes an infrastructure manager answerable to many customers and, from 2027, to a regulator.

## 3. Mining and industry

### Facts
- **Illegal mining costs about R21bn a year** (an older estimate sourced to the Minerals Council). The country has about 6,000 derelict or ownerless mines, and Operation Vala Umgodi, a joint police–army operation, targets four provinces ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zama_zama)).
- **The Stilfontein standoff:** police sealed supply routes to hundreds of illegal miners from Nov 2024. A court-ordered rescue in Jan 2025 brought up about 246 survivors and 78 bodies **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Anglo American's FutureSmart Mining** public page stresses tailings, leaching and renewables, not named digital vendors ([Anglo](https://www.angloamerican.com/futuresmart/futuresmart-mining)).
- **DataProphet (Cape Town)** sells AI process optimisation to manufacturers and foundries ([DataProphet](https://dataprophet.com/)).
- **[UNVERIFIED]:** the big SA miners (Sibanye-Stillwater, Harmony, Implats, Exxaro, Kumba) run SAP, AVEVA/OSIsoft PI plant-data historians, Deswik or Hexagon mine planning, and their own operations centres. We found no public Palantir or Cognite production deployments.

### Analysis
Miners already have platforms for plant data and their own data teams, which makes them a poor entry point. The gaps are:
- **security intelligence:** illegal-mining networks and copper-theft chains;
- **export logistics across many parties:** rail slots, port stockpiles and border trucking.

Miners are strong **second customers** for a corridor product.

- **Irregular spending:** R145.21bn of municipal irregular expenditure since 2021-22, 87% from breaking procurement rules ([FWdK](https://fwdeklerk.org/auditor-general-tsakani-maluleke-tabled-the-consolidated-report-on-local-government-audit-outcomes-2024-25-on-24-june-2026/)). ### Analysis: the procurement-integrity data model
Most losses to the state trace through the same connected records:
- **the money trail:** tender, bid, award, contract, variation, invoice, payment;
- **the people trail:** supplier, director or beneficial owner, shared address, phone or bank account, related parties, officials;
- **the consequences trail:** court judgments, debarment, AGSA findings, media.

Public data already covers much of this: eTenders' open-contracting feed, the central supplier database, CIPC company records, court judgments, the Government Gazette, AGSA reports and restricted-supplier lists. The most valuable missing links are payments, contract variations and employee–supplier conflicts. These sit in state-owned companies' ERP systems and can only be reached with a client.

This is MB's home ground. The political and litigation risk means every output must be evidence-grade, logged and reviewed by lawyers.

## 10. Wedge scoring

Scores run 1–5, and **5 is always best**: for sales cycle, 5 means shortest; for ethical and political risk, 5 means lowest risk. Weights: pain 20%, budget 20%, data access 15%, sales cycle 10%, MB's advantage 15%, expansion 10%, ethics 10%.

| **B. Procurement and counterparty graph** | 4 | 3 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 3 | **4.00** |

| K. Municipal revenue protection | 5 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 2.80 |

**The top three.**
1. **A: Corridor Integrity.**
   - **Why it wins:** a budgeted, measurable pain with a named owner. Transnet spends about R4bn on security yet loses 4.5 mt a year, and its CEO has said cutting cable theft by a third makes freight profitable.
   - **Timing:** TRIM is being corporatised, the fourth network statement is law, and the regulator arrives in 2027. MB already has the Transnet relationship.
   - **Expansion:** the same model for crime along long, linear assets carries over to Eskom and NTCSA, commuter rail (PRASA), Telkom, miners and municipalities.
   - **Risks:** Transnet's weak underlying finances, state procurement rules, data access, and insiders who may be complicit.
2. **B: Procurement and counterparty graph.**
   - **Why it's second:** it is the fastest to build on public data and plays to MB's strongest skills. Private buyers can pay within weeks.
   - **How it fits A:** it is A's foundation, since contractors, scrap dealers and suspects are all entities in the same graph.
   - **Risks:** government buyers pay less, and the political exposure is higher.
3. **C: Rail and port control tower.**
   - **Why it's third:** demand is private, solvent and time-bound, with operators on the main line from 2027.
   - **What it depends on:** TRIM sharing data, which A unlocks.

**Recommended first wedge: A, built on B's graph, with C as the first expansion.**

The first product is a "Corridor Integrity Room" for one or two corridors, with four parts:
- **An asset and incident twin:** track sections and cable runs, joined with incidents from Transnet security, contractors, drones and SAPS case numbers.
- **A party graph:** contractors, scrap buyers, vehicles (plate-reader hits) and companies, from CIPC, eTenders and court records.
- **Write-back into Transnet's own systems:** repair work orders into SAP, penalties against contractors' service levels, security dispatch, and prosecution-ready case files for police and prosecutors.
- **KPIs:** km of cable stolen, train-hours lost, tonnes lost, repair time and the arrest-to-conviction ratio.

## Implications for Vigil

1. **Start with a 90-day paid proof of value at Transnet and TRIM.** Cover one corridor and one crime type: copper cable and overhead line equipment. Agree baselines up front, and price as a platform fee plus a share of verified avoided losses. The ~R4bn security budget is the source of funds, not the IT budget.
2. **Make "from incident to conviction" the headline feature.** That means prosecution-ready case files with a documented chain of evidence, and tracking outcomes through SAPS and the prosecutors. No camera network, integrator or audit firm does this; it is where MB's legal practice gives Vigil an edge.
3. **Build the entity graph from public data now, and sell it this quarter** to banks, development lenders (Lobito, private transmission, BMA) and the 12 private rail operators. This revenue reduces dependence on Transnet's procurement timetable.
4. **Aim to be the neutral data layer for open access.** TRIM, the operators and the 2027 regulator all need one auditable record of capacity, availability, incidents and penalties. Win incidents first; capacity follows.
5. **Expand in the order that reuses the data model most:**
   1. rail;
   2. Eskom and NTCSA (grid crime, and the ~R17.5bn a year lost to theft);
   3. PRASA, Telkom and municipal cable theft;
   4. miners' export logistics and security;
   5. the BMA border concessionaires (sell to the private consortia, not the state);
   6. the Maputo, North–South and Lobito corridors.
6. **Say no for 2026–27 to municipal revenue collection, general policing and defence.** Municipalities can't pay, the ethical risk is high, and the sales cycles are too long.
7. **Write the ethics rules before the first contract:**
   - no facial recognition in version 1;
   - compliance with privacy and access-to-information law (POPIA and PAIA);
   - a human decides anything with consequences, and every action is logged;
   - an external advisory board;
   - a public contrast with Chinese safe-city projects.
8. **Keep Vigil's own procurement spotless.** Put a written conflict-of-interest barrier between MB's legal practice and Vigil while Transnet is a client. Win through an open tender, a pilot funded by business or a development lender (for example via the NLCC and BUSA), or an existing integrator contract. A procurement-integrity company cannot survive a hint of a sweetheart deal.
9. **Partner rather than fight.** Plug in Vumacam, vehicle-tracking data (Cartrack, Netstar, Tracker), drone suppliers, SAP and Esri. Sell through BCX, Altron or NTT Data where Transnet's rules require a large prime contractor, but keep the data model and IP.
10. **Treat data sovereignty and cost as the answer to Palantir.** Host in South Africa, use open standards (such as the open-contracting standard), let clients export their data, and price well below global platforms.
11. **Avoid Gro Intelligence's trap.** Don't build a general data product before an anchor customer, and keep spending tied to signed revenue.
12. **Watch these dates:**
    - now: the TRIM corporatisation task team (from Aug 2026);
    - late 2026 to early 2027: BMA border construction starts;
    - early 2027: external trading on the wholesale electricity market;
    - 30 Dec 2026: the EU deforestation rule applies to large operators;
    - Mar 2027: Eskom's smart-meter target falls due;
    - Apr 2027: the Transport Economic Regulator becomes operational.
