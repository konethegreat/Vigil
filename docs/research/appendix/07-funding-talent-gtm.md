### 1.3 South African public funding (facts)
- **s11D R&D incentive:** 150% deduction on operational R&D spend. Requires pre-approval from the Department of Science and Innovation. Available **until 31 Dec 2033** ([SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/businesses-and-employers/my-business-and-tax/research-and-development-incentive/)). Since 1 Jan 2024, three changes apply ([Tax Faculty](https://taxfaculty.ac.za/news/read/navigating-the-enhanced-section-11d-r-d-incentive)):
  - software development with systematic, uncertain-outcome work can qualify;
  - internal business processes are no longer excluded;
  - there is a six-month grace period to apply.
- **Technology Innovation Agency (TIA)** ([TIA](https://www.tia.org.za/funding-instruments/)):
  - Technology Development Fund (grant, TRL 4–7);
  - Commercialisation Support Fund (TRL 8–9);
  - Seed Fund;
  - **Technology Acquisition and Deployment Fund: up to R1m over 24 months for SA developers that already have government or municipal customers**;
  - Industry Matching Fund, which co-invests alongside VCs.
- **Jobs Fund** (est. 2011): private applicants must match 1:1. R4.62bn committed across 89 projects ([Jobs Fund](https://www.jobsfund.org.za/)).
- **NEF** supports black entrepreneurs; product details [UNVERIFIED] ([NEF](https://www.nefcorp.co.za/)).
- **DBSA** led a R50m round into Zimi Charge in June 2026 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/09/02/south-africa-startup-funding-recovering-founders-struggle/)). **PIC** anchors both Hlayisani and Mamor.
- IDC (including SPII), SEFA and DSI grant terms: the pages I read gave no usable detail [UNVERIFIED] ([IDC](https://www.idc.co.za/)).

### 1.5 Sovereign wealth fund (facts)
- The DBSA is running a feasibility study. The proposed mandate covers "infrastructure development, economic diversification, industrialisation, energy transition…".
- Possible funding sources are strategic minerals, pension capital (R5.6–5.8 trillion), SOE reform and other public assets. There is no launch date. A pan-African fund was also proposed during SA's G20 presidency ([TimesLive, 5 Sep 2026](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/business/2026-09-05-development-bank-of-southern-africa-moves-to-launch-sa-sovereign-wealth-fund/)).
- Bloomberg reported that the state property company is being considered as a route to a wealth fund (9 Mar 2026). I could not read the article ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-09/south-africa-sees-state-property-firm-as-path-to-wealth-fund)).
- **The draft National AI Policy was gazetted on 10 Apr 2026 and withdrawn on 26 Apr 2026 because it contained fictitious citations.** This comes from a single source ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_South_Africa_National_Artificial_Intelligence_(AI)_Policy_2026)).

**Analysis.** DFIs matter most as financiers of customers' projects, not as seed investors. The wealth fund won't invest before about 2028, but its SOE-reform language gives Vigil a hook: "the performance layer for state assets."

## 3. Palantir and Anduril financing, and a capital plan for Vigil

**Palantir (facts).**
- In-Q-Tel invested about $2M around 2004. Palantir had raised more than $2.3B by its September 2020 listing. It was unprofitable for about 20 years; its first GAAP-positive quarter was Q1 2023, with $31M net income ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palantir_Technologies)).
- From the S-1 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1321655/000119312520230013/d904406ds1.htm)):
  - 2018 revenue $595.4M with a net loss of $580.0M; 2019 revenue $742.6M with a net loss of $579.6M;
  - accumulated deficit of $3.96B at 30 June 2020;
  - pilots offered "at no or low cost";
  - **contribution margin rose from 14% (2018) to 21% (2019) and 48% (H1 2020)**;
  - the top 20 customers averaged **$24.8M** each in 2019.
- Forward-deployed engineers started at Palantir under the name "Delta". Until about 2016 Palantir employed more FDEs than regular software engineers ([Pragmatic Engineer, 12 Aug 2025](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/forward-deployed-engineers)).
- 2025 revenue was about $4.5B, 53.7% of it from government. Its AIP product is sold through five-day bootcamps ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palantir_Technologies)).

**Anduril (facts)** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anduril_Industries)):
- Incorporated April 2017 with a Founders Fund seed round.
- Rounds: $120M at more than $1B (Sep 2019); $200M at $2B (Jul 2020); $450M at $4.6B (Jun 2021); $1.5B at $14B (Aug 2024); $2.5B at $30.5B (Jun 2025).
- 2026 rounds were reported at $4B/$60B in March and $5B/$61B in May. That comes from one source and its round letters are inconsistent, so treat it as [UNVERIFIED]. A December 2022 round of about $1.48B is also [UNVERIFIED].
- Revenue went from about $100M (2020) to about $2B (2025).
- It builds products before the Pentagon asks for them, paying with VC money instead of cost-plus contracts, and is building a $1B Arsenal-1 factory in Ohio.

**Analysis: what carries over to Vigil.**
- Neither model can be copied: Palantir's pre-IPO funding alone was about 3.5 times all South African equity raised in 2025.
- Three things carry over: **a paying anchor customer in In-Q-Tel's role (Transnet)**; **Acquire, Expand, Scale with contribution margin tracked per customer**; and **Anduril's "build on our own dime", for software only**, as reusable rail, port, mine and municipal-revenue templates.

**Recommended capital plan (analysis).** Figures use about R16.1/$, implied by Grindstone's ZAR500m ≈ $31M in September 2026 ([Disrupt Africa](https://disruptafrica.com/2026/09/11/sas-grindstone-ventures-launches-new-31m-fund/)).

| Stage | Timing | Amount | Likely sources | Milestones needed to raise the next stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bootstrap | Months 0–9 | R8–15m | MB Tech balance sheet, paid Transnet work, TIA grants, s11D | One paid deployment live with write-back; 8–10 staff |
| Seed | Months 9–15 | $2–3.5M | Knife, Hlayisani, Grindstone, 4Di, HAVAIC, angels | R10–20m contracted ARR; customers in 2 sectors; ISO 27001 certified |
| Series A | Months 24–30 | $8–12M | Partech Africa, TLcom or Knife, plus IFC, BII or Proparco co-investing | R60–100m ARR; net revenue retention above 120%; first non-SA customer; SOC 2 Type II |
| Series B | Month 40+ | $25–40M | Gulf, US or DFI growth money | R200m+ ARR across 3 or more countries |

The fully bootstrapped alternative protects ownership but risks turning Vigil into an SI. **I recommend a hybrid: services no more than 40% of revenue by month 24, and contribution margin above 40%.** It took Palantir until 2020 to reach 48%.

## 4. Talent

**Facts.**
- **OfferZen 2025** (3,423 responses; fieldwork Sep–Nov 2024) ([OfferZen](https://www.offerzen.com/reports/software-developer-south-africa)):
  - Cape Town pays best, but Johannesburg gives 9.7% more purchasing power;
  - **Go is among the best-paid languages**, with Ruby and Kotlin;
  - fewer than 40% of roles are fully remote;
  - more than 50% of remote-first companies let staff work from anywhere;
  - 37.9% use coding assistants at work.
- **OfferZen 2026** (2,270 responses; Feb 2026) ([OfferZen](https://www.offerzen.com/resources/developer-salary-benefits-report)):
  - 49% of developers feel underpaid (62% of juniors);
  - 44% have spent time learning AI tools;
  - hiring is held back more by budgets (40%) than by a shortage of candidates (20%).
- **Base salaries in ZAR, 2026:**
  - PayScale, July 2026: software engineer average R410,541 (range R121k–R769k); entry level R292,925 ([PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/ZA/Job=Software_Engineer/Salary)). **Senior software engineer average R829,539** (R504k–R1.0m); 5–9 years' experience R804,213 ([PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/ZA/Job=Senior_Software_Engineer/Salary)). Data scientist R460,138 ([PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/ZA/Job=Data_Scientist/Salary)). ML engineer R428,920, from only 57 profiles ([PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/ZA/Job=Machine_Learning_Engineer/Salary)).
  - Indeed, 4 Sep 2026: senior R58,382/month; principal about R1.08m/year; **SITA is among the highest payers at R817,586/year** ([Indeed](https://za.indeed.com/career/software-engineer/salaries)).
- **Competition from dollar-paying employers:** Palantir's New York forward-deployed software engineer role pays **$135k–$200k base plus RSUs**, needs one year's experience and involves up to 25% travel ([Palantir/Lever](https://jobs.lever.co/palantir/dab396d4-2f14-4796-aac0-0d82883dccf0)). Palantir also advertises FDE roles in Abu Dhabi and Dubai ([Lever](https://jobs.lever.co/palantir)).
- **Forward-deployed engineers are in high demand:** OpenAI has 10+ FDEs across 8 cities; Ramp about 15; Salesforce and Gecko Robotics are also hiring; a16z has called it "the hottest job in tech" ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/forward-deployed-engineers)).
- **Communities:**
  - Deep Learning Indaba: Kigali in 2025, Nigeria in 2026 ([Indaba](https://deeplearningindaba.com/));
  - Masakhane: 1,000+ participants from 30 countries working on 38+ languages ([Masakhane](https://www.masakhane.io/));
  - Zindi: 100,000+ AI builders, now at zindi.world ([Zindi](https://zindi.world/));
  - ALX: 347.1K graduates ([ALX](https://www.alxafrica.com/));
  - Andela: 17,000 certified AI engineers ([Andela](https://www.andela.com/)).
- **Unverified:** CS and engineering graduate numbers for Wits, UCT, Stellenbosch, UP, UJ and UKZN; the size of SA's Rust and Elixir talent pool; emigration rates; and SSA vetting timelines and citizenship rules. SITA's standards refer to MISS, the Minimum Information Security Standards ([SITA](https://www.sita.co.za/)).

**Analysis.**
- A South African senior base (about R830k, roughly $52k) is 2.6–3.9 times below Palantir's New York base, so expect the top 10% to be poached. Counter with real equity, dollar-indexed staff-level bonuses, the sovereign mission and Gulf and pan-African deployments.

- **Vetting:** people working on classified deployments will need to be South African citizens who can be cleared [UNVERIFIED].

## 5. Go-to-market

**Routes into the state (facts).**
- The SITA Act splits SITA's services into mandatory and non-mandatory ones. SITA runs transversal contracts covering software, hardware, services, panels and OEM/OSM framework agreements, and applies the MIOS and MISS standards ([SITA](https://www.sita.co.za/)).
- SITA may act as procurement agency for government IT under section 7(1)(g) of the Act ([SITA Act](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a88-98.pdf)).
- The following are [UNVERIFIED]:
  - national and provincial departments must buy IT through SITA, while SOEs such as Transnet and Eskom and the metros buy under their own supply-chain policies;
  - Treasury caps on contract variations;
  - the rules for unsolicited proposals;
  - PPPFA 80/20 and 90/10 scoring;
  - when the Public Procurement Act 2024 comes into force.

**Routes ranked (analysis).**
1. **Grow the Transnet work:** paid bootcamp, then production pilot, then a formal platform tender.
2. **Subcontract through panel-holding integrators** (Altron, BCX, NTT Data, iOCO), keeping the IP and the customer.
3. **Metro deployments funded by TIA's deployment fund** (up to R1m each).
4. **SITA transversal or OEM listing**, which will take 12–24 months.
5. **Unsolicited proposals**, only as a last resort.

For private design partners, pick one mining house, one logistics or terminal operator, and one bank or insurer (fraud, claims or AML).

**B-BBEE (facts)** ([B-BBEE Commission](https://www.bbbeecommission.co.za/faqs/)):
- Size bands: EMEs have turnover below R10m; QSEs R10–50m; large enterprises above R50m.
- **EMEs are automatically Level 4. At least 51% black ownership gives Level 2, and 100% gives Level 1, proven by sworn affidavit or CIPC certificate.** QSEs that are at least 51% black-owned get the same treatment; other QSEs must be verified.
- "Black" means African, Coloured or Indian South African citizens, by birth, descent, or naturalisation before 27 April 1994.
- ICT Sector Code targets, recognition of ESOPs and broad-based schemes, and the fronting rules are [UNVERIFIED]. Confirm them with a verification agency.

**Analysis.** Keep Vigil at least 51% black-owned through Series A. To do that:
- hold the founders' shares in a black-owned holding company;
- set up a broad-based employee trust;
- take money from PIC-backed funds such as Hlayisani and Mamor and from Grindstone, which target black-owned companies;
- keep foreign investors at the holding-company level only if a verification agency confirms that won't cost points.

**Pricing benchmarks (facts, AWS Marketplace, Sep 2026):**
- **Palantir:** "private pricing only", sold on upfront commitment. AWS's review-sentiment summary scores its cost-effectiveness 53% negative ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-a5m5xespbqyci)).
- **Cognite Data Fusion:** upfront commitment, priced through a master subscription agreement order form, with value promised "in as little as eight weeks" ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-pnszk2onnidau)).
- **C3 AI:** sells an "Initial Production Deployment" completed "within up to six months" ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-vwfe5ctlxldjk)). Its move to consumption pricing is [UNVERIFIED].

**Proposed ZAR price points (analysis, excluding VAT):**

| Offer | Price |
|---|---|
| 3–5 day bootcamp, credited against a later subscription | R150–400k |
| 8–16 week pilot | R1.5–4m |
| Platform for one SOE division or one metro | R6–20m a year |
| Enterprise-wide agreement | R25–60m a year |
| FDE retainer | R2–3m per FDE-year |
| Compute and LLM usage | Passed through, plus 20–30% |

The ceiling for reference: Palantir's 2019 top-20 average of $24.8M is about R400m a year.

**Adapting the bootcamp model (analysis).** Make bootcamps **paid, on-site and offline-capable**: edge hardware, pre-built SAP, OSIsoft PI and OPC-UA connectors, two FDEs and one domain expert. Each should end with a live ontology, one write-back workflow and a rand business case. Free pilots, as Palantir ran them, are harder to accept inside SOE procurement [UNVERIFIED].

**South African govtech lessons (facts, then analysis).**
- **SARS eFiling** began in 2000 as a private system, was relaunched free by SARS in 2003 and taken in-house in 2010. By 2015/16 it handled **98.7% of all submissions** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS_eFiling)). Lesson: the state adopts private technology but may take it over, so contract for long-term licensing and transfer on your terms.
- **e-tolls** went live on 3 Dec 2013. Only about 20% of users paid, and they were scrapped on 11 Apr 2024. The R20bn debt was split 70% National Treasury and 30% Gauteng ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-toll_(South_Africa))). Lesson: public legitimacy matters more than technology, so avoid use cases that put citizens under surveillance.
- Home Affairs modernisation, SAPS IT failures and the GovChat v Meta competition case: details [UNVERIFIED]. GovChat's lesson is platform-dependency risk.

## Implications for Vigil

1. **Turn Transnet into Vigil's In-Q-Tel.** Get a paid, referenceable production deployment before raising more than seed money. South Africa's H1 2026 market ($83M) will not fund an idea at Palantir scale.
2. **Never run free pilots.** Charge for bootcamps and credit them against the subscription. That protects procurement integrity and shows the customer is serious.
3. **Make the forward-deployed engineer Vigil's defining role.** Pay top-decile rand salaries with US-dollar-indexed upside, and build a Vigil Fellows graduate pipeline with Jobs Fund co-funding.
4. **Treat Level 1 or Level 2 B-BBEE status as a competitive moat.** Design the cap table so that 51% black ownership survives to Series A, using a black-owned holding company, a broad-based employee trust and PIC-backed funds.
5. **Stop using "Vigil" publicly until it is cleared.** Altron's vigil.co.za and 3xLOGIC's VIGIL are red flags in exactly Vigil's market. A coined name is cheaper now than a rebrand in year three.
6. **Use system integrators for channel, not for IP.** Reach SITA and panel contracts through Altron, BCX or iOCO, but keep the licence and the customer.
7. **Sell outcomes in rand** (tonnes railed, wagon turnaround, revenue recovered), not "ontology."
8. **Enter defence and security last.** Clearances, arms-control rules and US and NATO investor exclusions mean SOEs and mining should come first. Build a classified enclave around month 30.
9. **Collect non-dilutive money deliberately:** s11D at 150%, TIA's development and deployment funds, and the Jobs Fund. Together these could plausibly cover R3–8m over 24 months (estimate).
10. **Pitch DFIs as the payers behind customers' programmes, not as equity.** Target World Bank-style digital-government projects outside South Africa by month 30.
11. **Plan the exit route early.** A US or UAE subsidiary may be needed (VoxCroft), and a strategic investor can become the buyer (InstaDeep).
12. **Guard legitimacy:** publish a data and AI ethics charter, certify ISO/IEC 42001 and avoid citizen surveillance early. e-tolls failed on consent, not technology.
13. **Measure yourself against Palantir's unit economics.** Target contribution margin above 40% and services below 40% of revenue by month 24.
